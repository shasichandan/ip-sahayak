## SECTION 3(p) — TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE EXCLUSION

**Statute:** Indian Patents Act, 1970 — Section 3(p).

**Meaning:** An invention which in effect is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components is not patentable.

**Key principles:**
* Mere traditional Ayurvedic formulations cannot be patented.
* To overcome Section 3(p), the applicant must demonstrate novel technical intervention, non-obvious delivery system, unexpected therapeutic synergism, or novel extraction method.
* CSIR-TKDL (Traditional Knowledge Digital Library) acts as prior art to cancel or prevent biopiracy patents globally.

**Keywords:** section 3p, traditional knowledge, patent exclusion, tkdl, biopiracy, సెక్షన్ 3(p), సాంప్రదాయ పరిజ్ఞానం, धारा 3(p)

---

## SECTION 3(e) — SYNERGY IN ADMIXTURES

**Statute:** Indian Patents Act, 1970 — Section 3(e).

**Meaning:** A substance obtained by a mere admixture resulting only in the aggregation of the properties of the components thereof or a process for producing such substance is not patentable.

**Requirement:** For polyherbal or herbal-synthetic combinations, applicants must provide experimental proof of unexpected synergism (e.g., Combination Index < 0.8) where the combined efficacy exceeds the additive sum of individual ingredients.

**Keywords:** section 3e, synergistic effect, admixture, combination index, సెక్షన్ 3(e), సినర్జీ, धारा 3(e)

---

## CSIR-TKDL — TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE DIGITAL LIBRARY

**Authority:** Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) & Ministry of AYUSH.

**Scope:** Over 4.5 lakh digitized formulations from classical Ayurvedic texts (Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, Ashtanga Hridaya, etc.) classified under IPC Class A61K 36.

**Function:** Acts as defensive prior art used by Patent Offices worldwide (EPO, USPTO, JPO, IPO) to prevent wrongful patenting of traditional Indian remedies.

**Keywords:** tkdl, csir-tkdl, classical texts, samhita, prior art, టికెడిఎల్, टीकेडीएल

---

## BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY ACT — NBA FORM III APPROVAL

**Statute:** Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (amended 2023) — Section 6.

**Mandate:** Prior written approval from the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) via Form III is mandatory before applying for or obtaining any intellectual property rights (patents) inside or outside India based on any research or information on a biological resource obtained from India.

**Exemption:** Codified traditional knowledge practiced locally, provided biological resources are not commercialized without compliance.

**Keywords:** nba, biological diversity, form iii, abs compliance, ఎన్‌బిఎ, జైవ వైవిధ్యం, एनबीए

---

## TRADEMARK CLASS 5 — AYURVEDIC BRANDING

**Statute:** Trade Marks Act, 1999 — Nice Classification Class 5.

**Scope:** Pharmaceuticals, medical preparations, Ayurvedic herbal formulations, dietetic substances adapted for medical use.

**Filing Procedure:** Form TM-A filed online via IP India CGPDTM portal. Brand names should be coined and distinctive to avoid Section 9 (descriptive) and Section 11 (conflict) objections.

**Keywords:** trademark, class 5, form tm-a, brand name, ayush brand, ట్రేడ్‌మార్క్, క్లాస్ 5, ट्रेडमार्क

## RULE 158B — AYUSH REGULATORY LICENSING

**Statute:** Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 — Rule 158B.

**Authority:** State Licensing Authority (AYUSH).

**Requirements:**
* Classical Ayurvedic Medicines: Manufactured strictly according to authoritative texts listed in the First Schedule of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act.
* Proprietary Ayurvedic Medicines: Require published scientific literature, safety data, pilot clinical trial evidence, or acute toxicity study reports to obtain manufacturing licenses.

**Keywords:** rule 158b, ayush license, proprietary ayurvedic medicine, classical medicine, రూల్ 158బి, అయుష్ లైసెన్స్, नियम 158B
